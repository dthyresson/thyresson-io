Doctorin' the Product / Last Train to Model Central

Change the song. Remix the ingredients.

Cursor Change the product
  • Seed: right founders, right thesis. The developer experience wasn't there yet.
  • GitHub had the world's code and Copilot arriving. They didn't take the moment. Cursor did.
  • Six months on CAD tooling went nowhere. They experimented their way to code; Cursor shipped in 2023.
  • Series A, months later: same founders, same thesis, dramatically better product.
  • SpaceX acquired Cursor for $60B.
Don't fall in love with your first idea.
What hypothesis are you treating as destiny — and how would you test it?
fal Change the market
  • Burkay and Gorkem built highly optimized Python data infrastructure: pipelines, analytics, ML training.
  • The tell: tldraw's “Make it Real.” Sketch on screen, image in milliseconds.
  • Their stack was built for generative models at scale. The KLF didn't invent sampling; fal didn't invent generative AI.
  • “Every pixel will be generated, not rendered.” They recombined what existed into a new market.
  • fal raised $1.3B at a $16B valuation.
Apply what you already know to a new opportunity.
What have you already built that could serve a different market?

What Time Is Fit?

Anticipate the next sound. Know your audience.

Turso The market catches up
  • Supabase won the last era by being all-in-one: Postgres, auth, storage, edge functions. Vibe-coding tools defaulted to it.
  • Turso bet on unlimited SQLite: one database per user, per agent, per task. A point of view, not a market.
  • Then AI Agents arrived, needing a database as easily as a file. Turso had the shape.
  • Last month: Supabase announced Turso acquisition.
Have a point of view before it's consensus.
The Manual can't say what people want in two years. What's your bet — early, or just wrong?
Workbrew Find the buyer
  • Developers love Homebrew. Corporate IT does not love employees installing whatever they want.
  • IT needs visibility, security, and control.
  • Workbrew built the business in that gap: developers use it, companies pay to manage it.
  • A good song nobody hears isn't a hit. Good software that never reaches its buyer isn't a business.
Find the people with the problem and the money.
What free tool would companies pay to bring under control — and what would each side say they're buying?

Justified & Validated?

Reinvent the instrument.

CAD has been one of the most entrenched software markets in the world for fifty years. Zoo never tried to replace it.
  • Proprietary file formats, workstation-bound execution, workflows never designed for cloud compute or machine learning.
  • Design data sits in static files. Hard to use as training data.
  • Zoo rebuilt the stack from first principles: KCL turns design intent into machine-readable code, and a GPU-backed geometry engine runs through an API.
  • It plugs into existing stacks, connecting design, analysis, and manufacturing. Blue Origin is already feeding millions of CAD files into a fine-tuned Text-to-CAD model.
  • Like Cursor, rebuild the workflow around the model. Don't just replace the instruments; change how people play.
You don't have to replace the incumbent to win. Take on a fifty-year-old category without a fifty-year fight.
What entrenched market would you build a layer underneath?
Become the layer it cannot build itself. Change what's possible, not just what's available.

No single formula. No finish line.
Jura, 1994 · the million-pound burn

In 1992, The KLF quit the music business. Two years later, as the K Foundation, they took a million pounds in cash to the Scottish island of Jura and burned it.

As a VC, I've seen plenty of startups burn through a million pounds — but rarely quite so efficiently.

A number-one record isn't a lasting career. PMF isn't a lasting company. The Manual might tell you how to make a hit. It can't tell you what is worth making.
You don't have to invent the instruments to change the music. But you do have to make something people want to hear.
  • Curiosity, imagination, taste, critical thinking, understanding people, changing your mind.
  • Not exclusive to liberal arts grads. Exactly what a liberal arts education builds.
  • No permission required. No invention required. Find collaborators and assemble existing ideas in surprising ways.
  • How do you know it's real fit, not early enthusiasm?
  • Pivot the product or the market? Cursor vs. fal.
  • What if the buyer and the user are far apart?
  • Is being early just another word for wrong?
