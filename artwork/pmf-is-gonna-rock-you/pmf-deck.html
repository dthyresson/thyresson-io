The Manual

“If you want to do something, go and do it. Don't wait to be asked.”

Bill Drummond, The KLF — The Manual, 1988
Little money or talentFive top-ten singles
  • An ex-label A&R man and a squat-dwelling guitarist.
  • Not virtuosos. Conceptualists who sampled and collided other people's records.
  • By 1991, the biggest-selling singles act in the world.
  • They never invented the instruments.
The same bookDifferent title
  • Silicon Valley has spent decades trying to write The Manual.
  • We call it product-market fit.
  • Does it work? Does anyone want it?
  • Do they use it, pay for it, and keep coming back?
You don't need to invent a new technology. Sometimes the opportunity is recognizing what existing technology makes possible.

Doctorin' the Product / Last Train to Model Central

Change the product. Change the market.

CursorChange the product
  • Seed: right founders, right thesis. The developer experience wasn't there yet.
  • GitHub had the world's code and Copilot arriving. They didn't take the moment. Cursor did.
  • Anysphere tried an AI email client, then AI CAD for mechanical engineers. The CAD tool was even called Cursor.
  • Series A, months later: same founders, same thesis, dramatically better product.
Your first idea is a hypothesis, not your destiny.
What hypothesis are you treating as destiny — and what would it take to test it this month?
falChange the market
  • Burkay and Gorkem built highly optimized Python data infrastructure: pipelines, analytics, ML training.
  • The tell: tldraw's “Make it Real.” Sketch on screen, image in milliseconds.
  • Their stack was exceptionally good at running generative models at scale. When Stable Diffusion took off, they pointed it there.
  • “Every pixel will be generated, not rendered.” The market showed up and they were already standing in it.
The best opportunity isn't always the one you set out to find.
What have you already built that you could point at a completely different market?

What Time Is Fit?

The market catches up. Find the buyer.

TursoThe market catches up
  • Supabase won the last era by being all-in-one: Postgres, auth, storage, edge functions. Vibe-coding tools defaulted to it.
  • Turso bet on unlimited SQLite: one database per user, per agent, per task. Multi-tenant, local-first.
  • At the time, millions of small databases was an emerging idea, not yet a market.
  • Then agents arrived, needing a database as easily as a file, cheap and on demand. Turso had the shape.
Sometimes you don't find the market. The market finds you.
What's your two-year bet — and how will you know if you're early or just wrong?
WorkbrewFind the buyer
  • Developers love Homebrew. Corporate IT does not love employees installing whatever they want.
  • IT needs visibility, security, and control.
  • Workbrew built the business in that gap: developers use it, companies pay to manage it.
  • Two groups look at the same product and see completely different problems.
The person who uses your product isn't always the person who pays for it.
What free tool would companies pay to bring under control — and what would each side say they're buying?
October 2026: the company that won the last era bought the company built for the next one.

Justified & Validated?

Don't replace the incumbent. Become the layer.

CAD has been one of the most entrenched software markets in the world for fifty years. Zoo never tried to replace it.
  • Proprietary file formats, workstation-bound execution, workflows never designed for cloud compute or machine learning.
  • Design data sits in static files. Hard to use as training data.
  • Zoo rebuilt the stack from first principles: KCL turns design intent into machine-readable code, and a GPU-backed geometry engine runs through an API.
  • It plugs into existing stacks, connecting design, analysis, and manufacturing. Blue Origin is already feeding millions of CAD files into a fine-tuned Text-to-CAD model.
  • The analogy to Cursor is direct: rebuild the workflow around the model instead of bolting AI onto the old stack.
Become the layer it cannot build itself.
You do not have to replace the incumbent to win the market. That is how you take on a fifty-year-old category without a fifty-year fight.
What's one entrenched market you would never try to replace — and what layer could you build underneath it?
This requires connecting the lifecycle, not just shipping a feature.
